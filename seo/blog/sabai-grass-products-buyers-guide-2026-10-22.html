What are Sabai grass products?

Sabai grass (Eulaliopsis binata) is called Sabai ghas in Odisha and Babui ghas in Bengal. It grows in the dry forest areas of Odisha, West Bengal, Jharkhand and Chhattisgarh and needs very little water. Artisans twist the blades into rope or coil and stitch them into shapes. How do you choose a good Sabai grass product?

  • Look at the weave. Tight, even coils with firm stitching are a good sign. Loose ends should be tucked in.
  • Match the shape to the job. Frames and lids keep their shape; open baskets are better for light items.
  • Expect small variations. Each piece is handmade, so size and colour can differ slightly from the photo.
  • Check the size on the product page before you order, especially for planters and boxes.

How is a Sabai grass product made?

The long blades are dried, then twisted into a rope or coiled by hand. The coils are stitched round and round into the shape of a basket, box, tray or plate, and the fibre can be dyed so artisans can make many colours. Because every coil is placed by hand, a piece can take real time to finish, and no two are exactly alike. In districts such as Mayurbhanj in Odisha and Purulia in West Bengal, Sabai weaving is an important livelihood, much of it done by women working from home, often organised through self-help groups.

How do you care for Sabai grass?

Dust or wipe with a dry cloth. Keep it away from long exposure to water and harsh sun; if a piece gets wet, dry it in shade. Store it in a dry, airy place.

Why choose Sabai grass over plastic or other fibres?

Sabai grass is strong, biodegradable and plastic-free. It grows with minimal irrigation and its roots help bind soil. Compared with other fibres, it is firm and coil-woven. If you are still deciding, our jute, water hyacinth, Sabai and cane comparison sets them side by side.
