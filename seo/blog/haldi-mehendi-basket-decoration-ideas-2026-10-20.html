The simplest way to decorate baskets for Haldi and Mehendi is to keep the weave visible and add colour with flowers, fabric and ribbon: yellow and marigold for Haldi, green and pink for Mehendi. A handwoven grass or cane basket then works as the decor, the packing and a reusable return gift in one.

Why use baskets for Haldi and Mehendi ceremonies?

Both functions run on small, repeated moments: handing out flowers, offering haldi, giving favours to guests. A basket holds all of that, photographs well, and guests can reuse it at home. A natural-fibre basket is also plastic-free and works against almost any outfit colour.

How do you decorate a basket for a Haldi ceremony?

Which colours and flowers suit Haldi?

Stay with yellow, orange and white. Marigold garlands tied around the rim, a few jasmine strands and a small bundle of fresh leaves are enough. Let the natural straw show through, because it matches the warm tones.

What can go inside a Haldi basket?

  • Fresh petals for the guests to throw or tuck into hair.
  • Small bowls or a cloth pouch for haldi paste.
  • A thin cotton cloth in yellow as a liner.

How do you decorate baskets for a Mehendi ceremony?

Which colours suit Mehendi?

Green, pink and orange. Wrap the handle with a green ribbon or dupatta strip, add a few bangles or small tassels, and tie a tag with the guest's name.

What can go inside a Mehendi basket?

  • Mehendi cones, set out for the guests.
  • Bangles, a hair accessory or a small sweet box as the favour.
  • Tissue or shredded paper in a matching shade so the weave stays the star.

How do you set up the baskets step by step?

  1. Count your guests first. Decide how many favour baskets you need, plus a few extra for family and helpers.
  2. Pick one colour story per ceremony. Yellow and white for Haldi, green and pink for Mehendi keeps the table tidy in photographs.
  3. Line each basket. Use cloth or tissue so the weave stays clean.
  4. Fill and tag. Add the favour, then a small tag with a name or a thank-you line.
  5. Dress the rim last. Tie flowers, ribbon or a small tassel just before the guests arrive so fresh flowers stay fresh.

What are good ideas by budget?

Small favour baskets

Choose the small grass or straw baskets (from ₹550 each on our website) and fill them with one sweet box, a hair accessory or a few bangles. A ribbon and a tag are enough decoration.

Hampers for close family

Choose a medium basket or a cane hamper basket and layer in dry fruits, a candle, a small decor piece and a handwritten note. Cane holds its shape well, so it also works as a display piece on a side table.

Decor-only baskets

Baskets do not have to be gifts. Use larger ones to hold petals at the entrance, to stack Mehendi cones, or to carry the haldi bowls to the stage.

What do guests do with the basket afterwards?

This is the main reason to prefer woven baskets over a one-use box. Guests can keep fruit, toiletries, keys, toys or yarn in them. It is a gift that stays in the house and is noticed again.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Overfilling a small basket until the weave strains.
  • Putting wet flowers or paste directly on the straw.
  • Leaving baskets in damp storage after the function.
  • Ordering at the last minute. Wedding season runs from mid-November to mid-December, so plan early.

How do you choose and care for the baskets?

  • Match the size to the contents. Small for a favour and a sweet, medium for several items, cane for gifts to family.
  • Keep them dry. Wipe with a dry or barely damp cloth and keep away from standing water.
  • Use a liner. A cloth or paper liner protects the weave from haldi stains and mehendi paste.
  • Store flat and dry after the function; they can be reused for storage, fruit or festival gifting.

Frequently asked questions

What should I put in a Haldi ceremony basket?

Fresh petals, a small bowl for haldi paste and a yellow cloth liner are the usual basics, with a favour for guests if the basket doubles as a return gift.

What colours are best for Mehendi decoration?

Green, pink and orange are the most common. Natural straw looks good with all three.

Can I use one basket for both ceremonies?

Yes. Change the ribbon and flowers: marigold and yellow for Haldi, green and pink for Mehendi.

Are the baskets reusable?

Yes. They are handwoven from natural grass, straw or cane and can be reused for storage or gifting once the function is over.

How do I keep haldi from staining the basket?

Line it with cloth or paper, and keep wet paste in a bowl inside rather than directly on the weave.
