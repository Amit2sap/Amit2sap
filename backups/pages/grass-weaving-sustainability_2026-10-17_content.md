## Origin and Availability in India
Sabai grass is a perennial grass found abundantly in eastern and central India, especially in Odisha, West Bengal, Jharkhand, and Chhattisgarh. Locally known as Sabai Ghas in Odisha and Babui Ghash in Bengal, this fibrous plant has been used for generations.
The grass grows naturally in dry forested areas, requiring minimal water and virtually no chemical intervention—making it ideal for sustainable harvesting. Once used by the British Army for rope-making during World War II due to its strength, Sabai grass has since evolved into a valuable raw material for handicrafts.
## Strength and Benefits of Sabai Grass
Sabai grass is prized for its resilience, eco-friendliness, and versatility, making it a favorite among rural artisans and environmentally conscious consumers alike.
Key Benefits: Durability: Its strong natural fibers can withstand years of use, making it suitable for utility and décor products. / Biodegradable: 100% plastic-free, Sabai products decompose naturally and don't pollute. / Low Water Consumption: The grass grows with minimal irrigation, helping conserve water. / Craft-Friendly: It can be dyed, twisted, coiled, or braided, giving artisans limitless design possibilities.

## Empowering Artisans and Supporting Livelihoods
In remote districts like Mayurbhanj (Odisha) and Purulia (West Bengal), Sabai grass weaving has become a vital livelihood for thousands of tribal women. Self-help groups and local NGOs train women in weaving techniques and entrepreneurial skills, helping them transition from subsistence labor to sustainable income generation.
