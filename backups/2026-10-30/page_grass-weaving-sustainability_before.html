What is Sabai grass?

Sabai grass is a perennial grass that grows in eastern and central India, especially Odisha, West Bengal, Jharkhand and Chhattisgarh. It grows naturally in dry forest areas, needs very little water and almost no chemical inputs, which makes it a sustainable craft fibre. Its long, tough blades are twisted into rope or coiled and stitched into baskets and other products.

Benefits of Sabai grass

  • Durable: strong natural fibres that last for years in daily use.
  • Biodegradable and plastic-free: Sabai products break down naturally at the end of their life.
  • Low water use: the grass grows with minimal irrigation.
  • Craft-friendly: it can be dyed, twisted, coiled or braided, so artisans can make many shapes and colours.
  • Good for land: its roots help bind soil.

The artisans behind Sabai craft

In districts such as Mayurbhanj (Odisha) and Purulia (West Bengal), Sabai weaving is an important livelihood, much of it done by women working from home, often organised through self-help groups. GrassCrafts works with these artisans to bring their work to buyers across India.

How to care for Sabai grass products

Dust or wipe with a dry cloth. Keep away from long exposure to water and harsh sun; if a piece gets wet, dry it in shade. Store in a dry, airy place.

FAQ

What is Sabai grass called in English?

Its botanical name is Eulaliopsis binata. In English it is usually just called Sabai grass; it is known as Sabai ghas in Odisha and Babui ghas in Bengal.

Where does Sabai grass grow in India?

Mainly in Odisha, West Bengal, Jharkhand and Chhattisgarh, in dry forest areas.

What are Sabai grass products used for?

Storage baskets, gift boxes, bags, wall décor, planters, trays and table mats – all plastic-free and biodegradable.
