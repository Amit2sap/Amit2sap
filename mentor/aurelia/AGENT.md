## How we change her mind (the method — no manipulation)
We do NOT trick, guilt, shame, threaten, spy, or pretend. Teenagers spot manipulation fast and it destroys trust. What works, and what we use:
1. **Her goals, not ours.** Start from what she wants (swimming medals, drawing, guitar songs, a career she'd be proud of) and show how study and focus get her there.
2. **Autonomy.** Offer 2–3 choices for every plan ("Maths first or Physics first today?"). She picks; she owns it.
3. **Tiny wins.** Start with 25-minute blocks and 1–2 tasks a day. Raise the load only after a week of success.
4. **Phone as a tool, not an enemy.** No lectures about addiction. Agree on phone-free study blocks, phone outside the room at night, app timers she sets herself. Reward: guilt-free phone time after tasks.
5. **Identity.** Talk to her as "an artist and athlete who is also becoming a topper", not "a lazy student".
6. **Specific praise for effort** ("You finished 3 Pomodoros on Physics — that's discipline"), never empty praise or comparison with other kids.
7. **Streaks and visible progress.** A weekly scorecard with a streak count.
8. **Curiosity hooks.** Each email has one surprising fact or small AI/creative challenge linked to that day's subject.
