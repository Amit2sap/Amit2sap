# Information gain: how GrassCrafts writes content (owner, 29 Oct 2026)

**The idea.** Information gain means our content adds something new to what is already online, instead of repeating it. Google and the AI assistants look for content that feels new, specific and human, because readers do. The internet is full of AI-written pages that say the same thing ("jute is eco-friendly", "10 Diwali gift ideas"). Those pages don't rank and don't get cited.

**What only we can say** (AI can't invent it, and neither may we):
- **Opinions**: what we, as makers, think buyers get wrong (which grass suits which use, when not to buy a product).
- **Stories**: how a product is made, who makes it, why a basket is woven where it is.
- **Failures**: what didn't work for us and what we changed.
- **Experiences**: what customers and businesses actually asked us, ordered, or complained about.
- **Lessons learned**: what 11 years of making and selling taught us.

## The rule that comes first
Every story, number and quote must be **true and on record**: in `marketing/story_bank.md` (verified section), Shopify product data, or a repo file. If a piece needs a detail we don't have, write `[ASK SRISTI: …]` in the draft and add the question to the story bank. Never fill the gap with a plausible guess, never invent a customer, artisan name, quote, number or event. Customers are named only with their written permission; otherwise "a buyer in Maharashtra".

## The checklist for every piece (blog, page, post, pin, email, GBP post)
1. **The new thing.** Write in one line what this piece says that the top 5 Google results and a ChatGPT answer for the same question do not. If there is nothing, don't publish: find the angle first.
2. **At least one first-hand element**, from the story bank: an opinion, a making detail, a real customer question, a lesson, our own data.
3. **Specifics over adjectives.** Real sizes, real prices from Shopify, real materials, real places (only places confirmed in the story bank), real occasions. Cut "eco-friendly, sustainable, premium, unique" unless a fact follows.
4. **A human voice.** First person plural ("we", "our weavers"), or Sristi's voice in quotes only when the quote is in the story bank.
5. **Show the trade-off.** Say when a product is the wrong choice (e.g. a grass basket near constant damp). Honest limits build trust and are information competitors don't give.
6. **Answer the real question first** (for AI search): the first 2 sentences answer the buyer's question plainly; the story follows.
7. **No AI slop markers**: no "In today's world", "elevate your space", "look no further", no listicles of generic tips.

## Where each agent uses it
- **Website / blog**: every article opens with a first-hand angle (a customer question we were asked, a making detail, a mistake buyers make). One "From our workshop" paragraph per article from the story bank.
- **AI Search**: answer pages lead with the direct answer, then a fact only GrassCrafts can state (GI no. 567 for Madurkathi, made by women artisan communities, raw material decides where a product is made).
- **Social**: one story post per posting day: a making detail, a real question from a Live, a lesson. Captions end with a real question to the audience.
- **LinkedIn**: founder/owner lessons (what worked and what failed in selling handmade to businesses), in Amit's or Sristi's voice, from our own data.
- **Email & Actions**: the first line of a pitch states something specific we noticed about them, and one line of our own experience (e.g. what festive hamper buyers asked us this season), no generic praise.
- **Google Business**: posts describe the actual product with a making or use detail, not slogans.

## Feeding the story bank
- The Email & Actions run adds **one story question** to Sristi's morning email each day (from the "To collect" list). Her answers go into the verified section with the date.
- Agents add real customer questions (from Lives, comments, enquiry forms) to the "Real questions" list, anonymised.
